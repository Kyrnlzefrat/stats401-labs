4. Design and Comparison

The choropleth makes the United States and China visually prominent through the darkest colors while preserving the familiar outlines and geographic positions of countries. In the cartogram, the same economies become especially visually dominant because their areas are enlarged in proportion to GDP. By contrast, geographically large countries such as Russia, Canada, and Australia occupy much less visual area relative to their physical size because their nominal GDP is smaller than their geographic footprints. The choropleth therefore preserves boundaries, relative location, adjacency, and recognizable country shapes, making it useful for questions about where large economies are located and how economic values vary across regions. The cartogram deliberately distorts shape and geographic area, so it is harder to estimate real geographic extent or recognize some countries. However, area encoding makes differences in economic magnitude more salient, supporting comparisons of the relative size of economies. Switching from color to area changes the visual perception from where high values are located toward how much visual space each economy represents. The two views therefore emphasize different tasks while using the same underlying data.
