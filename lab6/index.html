Design Description

I chose rectangle area to represent GDP because larger GDP values can be shown as larger rectangles, making the relative economic size of countries easy to compare. I use color to represent GDP status, with green for Increase, gray for Unchanged, and red for Decrease. This allows viewers to quickly identify the direction of GDP changes while using area for the quantitative comparison. The nested rectangles and labels show the hierarchy from Continent to Area to Country. The two treemaps use Squarify and Binary segmentation methods. Squarify tries to produce rectangles with aspect ratios closer to squares, so country rectangles are generally easier to compare and their labels are easier to read. In contrast, Binary recursively divides the available space into two groups, resulting in a different spatial organization and potentially more elongated rectangles. Although both treemaps encode the same GDP values and statuses, the different segmentation strategies change the visual appearance and affect how easily the hierarchy and country sizes can be interpreted.
